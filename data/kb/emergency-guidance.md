# Dental Emergency Guidance

This page gives general guidance. It is not medical advice, and it does not replace a call to a dentist or to 911.

## When to call 911 or go to the emergency room

Go to the nearest hospital emergency room, or call 911, if you have:

- Swelling of the face or mouth that makes it hard to breathe or swallow
- Bleeding that does not stop after 15 minutes of firm pressure
- Injury to the jaw, or a suspected broken jaw
- Fever together with facial swelling

## Same-day emergency appointments

Call the clinic as soon as it opens. We keep two same-day emergency slots free at each clinic every day. An emergency exam is $135, and any treatment is charged on top of that.

## After-hours emergency line

Outside clinic hours, call (416) 555-0199 and leave a message with your name and number. The line is checked every two hours until 10 pm. If you are in severe pain at night and cannot wait, go to an emergency room.

## Knocked-out tooth

1. Pick the tooth up by the crown (the white part), not the root.
2. If it is dirty, rinse it gently with milk or saline for a few seconds. Do not scrub it.
3. If you can, put it back in the socket and bite gently on a clean cloth. If not, keep it in a small container of milk.
4. Call us straight away. A tooth has the best chance of being saved if it is treated within one hour.

## Toothache

Rinse with warm salt water, floss gently to remove any trapped food, and take over-the-counter pain relief as directed on the package. Do not put aspirin directly on the gum. Call us if the pain lasts more than a day.

## Broken tooth, lost filling or lost crown

Keep any piece that came out. Cover a sharp edge with sugar-free gum or dental wax. For a lost crown, a pharmacy sells temporary dental cement. Call us the same day.
